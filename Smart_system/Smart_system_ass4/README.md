### Improved CNN Models (Tutorial Section 45, Full CIFAR-10)

| Model | Params | Epochs | Train (s) | Accuracy | Macro F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1  Conv+ReLU+Pool | 72,730 | 10 | 19.5 | 0.6955 | 0.7001 |
| M2  + BatchNorm | 72,954 | 10 | 22.1 | 0.7486 | 0.7483 |
| M3  + Residual | 75,786 | 10 | 27.6 | 0.7574 | 0.7634 |
| M4  + Attention(SE) | 78,614 | 10 | 38.3 | 0.7525 | 0.7557 |

Every architecture in this progression integrates exactly one additional mechanism:

- **M1** — Baseline convolutional backbone (`Conv + ReLU + MaxPool`)
- **M2** — `+ BatchNorm`, stabilizing intermediate activation distributions and curbing internal covariate shift
- **M3** — `+ Residual connections` ($Y = \mathcal{F}(X) + X$), establishing unobstructed skip pathways for gradient backpropagation and signal propagation
- **M4** — `+ Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention`, adaptively recalibrating cross-channel feature responses

**Incremental structural sophistication does not automatically ensure generalization.** Incorporating Batch Normalization produced the single most decisive performance surge (+5.31% accuracy, rising from 69.55% to 74.86%) at an almost negligible cost of 224 parameters. Adding residual skip connections contributed an extra +0.88% boost (reaching 75.74%); across a shallow three-stage depth, signal degradation remains minimal. Most instructively, channel-wise attention failed to deliver test gains: M4 slightly trailed M3 by -0.49% (75.25% vs. 75.74%) despite achieving the lowest empirical training loss (0.4335), serving as a textbook illustration of overfitting on narrow channel representations without explicit regularizing penalties.

This substantiates a fundamental engineering guideline: "A novel architecture is simply a prior model augmented with an inductive mechanism targeted at a concrete operational defect." Architectural add-ons yield measurable value only when the network encounters the precise computational bottleneck they are engineered to resolve; otherwise, they impose unnecessary computational and parameter overhead.

## Core Analytical Findings

**1. Architectural equivalence transcends software frameworks.** Across three benchmark datasets and diverse network topologies, NumPy, Keras, and PyTorch exhibited identical parameter counts, exact layer-by-layer tensor dimensions, and test classification metrics within statistical seed variance. `S.Dense`, `keras.layers.Dense`, and `nn.Linear` compute functionally indistinguishable mathematical operations.

**2. Abstraction alters inspection capability rather than computational mechanics.** The native NumPy implementation exposes each phase of the `Forward -> Loss -> Gradient -> Update` pipeline to explicit examination, with hand-coded analytical gradients checked against central finite differences. Keras concentrates execution within an opaque `fit()` call, whereas PyTorch offers a modular imperative loop driven by dynamic autograd.

**3. Hardware execution efficiency is intrinsically workload-dependent.** For lightweight tabular MLPs, PCI-e data marshaling and CUDA kernel launch latencies render PyTorch GPU execution slower than optimized CPU NumPy. On multi-channel spatial convolutions, highly parallelized GPU tensor cores dominate, drastically reversing runtime efficiency.

**4. Inductive bias must faithfully mirror intrinsic data topology.** Tabular features exhibit no canonical spatial ordering, making fully connected MLPs the natural choice. Image tensors possess spatial locality and shift invariance, providing rigorous theoretical justification for local receptive fields and weight sharing via CNNs.

## Methodological Limitations

- Constrained training budget (5 epochs on subsets, 10 epochs on channel-constrained 16/32/64 stages for M1..M4) devoid of data augmentation or adaptive learning rate schedulers.
- Single stochastic seed across mainline comparisons. As established in Notebook 04, initialization noise accounts for up to ~0.05 accuracy variation, demonstrating that cross-framework disparities reside within statistical noise.
- CUDA acceleration on desktop GPU (RTX 3060 12GB) paired with native FP32/AMP tensor execution.
- Clinical 30-day readmission prediction remains an inherently challenging tabular task; modest macro-F1 scores reflect acute class imbalance and clinical label noise rather than optimization shortcomings.
